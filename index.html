01Overhead plan & mapping viewed from the floor

10 groups: 2 for JESUS · 3 star groups · 5 arcs. JESUS splits into two groups along an arc boundary — hide it by matching each half to its arc, reveal it by lighting both a shared color. This is the audience's view — looking up at the underside from the floor — so the word reads correctly here; the controller pixel-map (defined from above) is its left↔right mirror, applied when the map is cut. Row 1 is the upstage row (over the stage, drawn at the bottom); row 38 is downstage (over the crowd, at the top). Rows 33–38 taper to fit the shorter installed wires.

Reading the demo: waves roll upstage→downstage across the 5 arcs, the 3 star groups twinkle out of phase, then JESUS reveals — watch it lift out of the arcs and settle back in. Use JESUS hidden / shown to compare directly, and Group map for the raw assignment. The faint grey line behind each row is that row's installed wire rope, so you can see the taper against the real hardware.

Why these exact numbers: this design is hard-coded — there are no sliders anywhere in this document, so nobody can nudge it by accident. The arcs are concentric about a point 16 rows behind the upstage edge (the "tight rainbow" look), but their five widths are deliberately not quite equal (18 · 16 · 26 · 18 · 22% of the span) and the three star groups are weighted 110 · 60 · 60: neither is visible from the floor, and together they balance the ten controller zones so that every zone keeps at least 36 spare LEDs against its stock. Camouflage is 97% — the word crosses four arcs, but 251 of its 260 fixtures sit in the two it wears. The page recomputes the map from these constants on load and checks it against the locked digest; if anyone edits a constant, the banner above turns red.

03Rigging & fishing-line cut list

One wire rope per row is already installed level across the width at the deck; each LED drops on its own fishing line. Rows 1–32 carry 52 LEDs; the tapered rows 33–38 carry 44 · 40 · 36 · 32 · 28 · 24.

Ordering allowance: add knot/tie-off to every drop (~10–15%). Row 1 = upstage (longest line, 19′ 0″); row 38 = downstage (shortest, 6′ 8″). Every row is exactly 4″ shorter than the one before — the same ladder as the original 40-row list; its 6′ 0″ and 6′ 4″ lengths are simply no longer used. Each length is used once per LED in that row — the LEDs column is the multiplier.

06Row-by-row hang guide volunteer build sequence · locked design

One strip per wire rope, Row 1 (upstage, over the stage) through Row 38 (downstage, over the crowd). Positions run 1–52 left to right, exactly as drawn in the overhead plan above; on the tapered rows 33–38 the outer positions are left empty (dashed) so the columns stay lined up with the rows above — the header of each strip says where its first light goes. Each numbered square is one LED — the number is the controller zone (= the stock bin) to pull from. The line under each strip reads the same sequence as counts: 6×Z2 means “six lights from Zone 2 in a row.” Line lengths match the cut list.

Zone stock limits: Zones 1–2 hold 100 · Zones 3–4 hold 400 · Zones 5–6 hold 300 · Zones 7–8 hold 210 · Zones 9–10 hold 150. The legend reads count/stock · spare — teal = 20 or more spare LEDs, gold = under 20 spare, red = over stock.

Orientation check before hanging: this guide (like the overhead plan) is the audience’s view from the floor, looking up. If you lay wires out on the deck or work from above, left↔right is mirrored — agree on which physical end of each wire is position 1 before attaching the first light. The tapered rows are centered on their shorter wires — measure from the wire's midpoint, not its end. Each strip's header gives the distance from the wire end to its first light (about 3½″ on the full rows; 7″ on row 33, 3′ 1″ on row 34, 10½″ on row 35, then 3′ 4½″ / 5′ 10½″ / 8′ 4½″ on rows 36–38).
